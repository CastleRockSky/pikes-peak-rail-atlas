Pikes Peak Rail Atlas

Murphy

stop, 1900 to 1945

Murphy was a small station on the Midland Terminal Railway between Divide and Midland. The Midland Terminal's public timetables of September 1920 to January 1945 print it 30.4 miles from Colorado Springs (3.5 miles beyond Divide, 2.7 short of Midland), with no altitude and no telegraph or coupon sign; those of 1935 and 1940 mark its trains 'f', stops on signal. The 1909 table does not list it, nor does that of March 1948. It was older than the tables show: newspapers report a freight train derailed 'at Murphy' in June 1900, a passenger train running into the rear of a standing ore train 'at Murphy, three miles south of Divide' in August 1901, and hail 'at Murphy, a small station on the Midland Terminal road' in July 1907. At the printed mileage the old grade, named 'OLD RAILROAD GRADE' on the 1954 USGS map and now partly a trail in Mueller State Park, runs as a narrow side-hill bench up the west side of a valley; about 0.1 mile further on it swings round a sharp bend on a fill below Tracy Hill and passes through a cut over the summit of the line between Divide and Midland, about 9,390 ft on the lidar. No siding, platform, building site or other trace of a station has been found on the ground, and no map held prints the name. What the stop served is not known.

Still open

  • Where exactly the stop was. The point is the printed mileage only. Midland's own mileage is in dispute (6.2 printed against 6.88 to the settlement along the route); if 6.2 marks the settlement, Murphy falls about 0.36 mile further south, beyond the summit. The summit of the line (0.17 mile past the point) and the bend where a road came in on the 1954 map are the obvious candidates, but nothing confirms either.
  • Whether Murphy had a siding. The 1901 wreck, with an ore train standing at Murphy, suggests trains waited there, but the side-hill bench read 3 to 6 m wide from about 0.2 mile before the point to 0.3 mile beyond it, and no widening was found. The bench west of the creek further north has not been measured across for one. A railway track chart, a valuation map (Interstate Commerce Commission, about 1917 to 1920) or an employee timetable would settle it.
  • What Murphy served and why it was named: ranch, timber, ice, a section house, or simply the summit. No post office of the name is listed in Colorado (Wikipedia's list), no Murphy feature was found near here in a web search of gazetteer listings, and the library catalogues returned nothing. The 1954 sheet marks a well and three buildings near the grade; their use and date are not known.
  • The 1900 Victor Record calls Murphy 'the next station north of Gillett', which does not fit the later tables (Midland and, until 1933, Waters lie between). Midland had lost its post office in 1895; whether it was still a station in 1900 is not known.
  • When Murphy opened and closed. First seen in June 1900 (newspaper); printed in public timetables from September 1920 (no table between 1909 and 1920 is held) to January 1945; gone by March 1948. The September 1909 table, which lists only Midland between Divide and Gillett, omits it, although the 1907 newspaper calls it a station: public tables evidently left minor stops out.
  • The route through this stretch was corrected on 24 Nov 2026 at the owner's request (once_route_mt_murphy.py): between about 2.25 and 3.31 route miles it had lain on the creek's flat floor, with a kink, and now follows the bench west of the creek; that stretch and the bench on to the summit cut are graded measured. The route north of 2.25, on or beside the present highway, is still inferred: which side of the highway the rails ran is not settled.
  • Searches that found nothing (24 Nov 2026): Denver Public Library digital collections ('Murphy Midland Terminal', 'Murphy Divide Colo', 'Murphy siding railroad', 'Murphy Teller County', 'Place Names Colorado Murphy', 'Tracy Hill Teller', 'MT train Murphy', 'Perry MT train near Divide'; results are swamped by city directories and people called Murphy); Pikes Peak Library District ('Murphy Divide', 'Murphy Midland Terminal', 'Murphy Teller'; the only lead, photographs of a ranch homesteaded by a Patrick Murphy, is the Pettigrew Ranch on the Palmer Divide in El Paso County, not this place); Colorado Historic Newspapers ('Murphy siding', 'Murphy spur', 'Murphy switch', 'Murphy station' Divide: other places). Otto Perry's photographs 'near Divide' of January 1949 (DPL OP-12726 and others) have not been placed and may or may not show this stretch.
