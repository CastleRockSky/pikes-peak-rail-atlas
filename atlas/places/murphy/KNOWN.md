# Murphy: what is known and how well

Generated from the atlas records by `atlas/tools/summary.py`. Do not edit by hand; change the records and run it again.

Murphy was a small station on the Midland Terminal Railway between Divide and Midland. The Midland Terminal's public timetables of September 1920 to January 1945 print it 30.4 miles from Colorado Springs (3.5 miles beyond Divide, 2.7 short of Midland), with no altitude and no telegraph or coupon sign; those of 1935 and 1940 mark its trains 'f', stops on signal. The 1909 table does not list it, nor does that of March 1948. It was older than the tables show: newspapers report a freight train derailed 'at Murphy' in June 1900, a passenger train running into the rear of a standing ore train 'at Murphy, three miles south of Divide' in August 1901, and hail 'at Murphy, a small station on the Midland Terminal road' in July 1907. At the printed mileage the old grade, named 'OLD RAILROAD GRADE' on the 1954 USGS map and now partly a trail in Mueller State Park, runs as a narrow side-hill bench up the west side of a valley; about 0.1 mile further on it swings round a sharp bend on a fill below Tracy Hill and passes through a cut over the summit of the line between Divide and Midland, about 9,390 ft on the lidar. At the printed mileage itself the bench widens, for about 330 ft, to a flat 13 to 20 m across, room for a second track or a loading ground; nowhere else between the highway and the summit does it widen for long. What the flat was, and what the stop served, are not known, and no map held prints the name.

## Still open

- Where exactly the stop was. The point is the printed mileage only. Midland's own mileage is in dispute (6.2 printed against 6.86 to the settlement along the route); if 6.2 marks the settlement, Murphy falls about 0.35 mile further south, beyond the summit. The summit of the line (0.15 mile past the point) and the bend where a road came in on the 1954 map are the obvious candidates, but nothing confirms either.
- Whether Murphy had a siding. The bench was measured across every 5 m from the highway to the summit cut: it reads mostly 4 to 12 m (the measure catches its shoulders), and widens only once for any length, to a flat 13 to 20 m across for about 330 ft at the printed mileage (record widened-flat). That is room for a second track a few cars long, or a loading ground, but too short for a passing siding for ore trains; the 1901 wreck, an ore train standing at Murphy run into from behind, does not need one. The 150 m past the pond, where a dam and basin have disturbed the ground, cannot be read. A railway track chart, a valuation map (Interstate Commerce Commission, about 1917 to 1920) or an employee timetable would settle it.
- What Murphy served and why it was named: ranch, timber, ice, a section house, or simply the summit. No post office of the name is listed in Colorado (Wikipedia's list), no Murphy feature was found near here in a web search of gazetteer listings, and the library catalogues returned nothing. The 1954 sheet marks a well and three buildings near the grade; their use and date are not known.
- The 1900 Victor Record calls Murphy 'the next station north of Gillett', which does not fit the later tables (Midland and, until 1933, Waters lie between). Midland had lost its post office in 1895; whether it was still a station in 1900 is not known.
- When Murphy opened and closed. First seen in June 1900 (newspaper); printed in public timetables from September 1920 (no table between 1909 and 1920 is held) to January 1945; gone by March 1948. The September 1909 table, which lists only Midland between Divide and Gillett, omits it, although the 1907 newspaper calls it a station: public tables evidently left minor stops out.
- The route through this stretch was corrected on 24 Nov 2026 at the owner's request (once_route_mt_murphy.py): between about 2.25 and 3.28 route miles it had lain on the creek's flat floor, with a kink, and now follows the bench west of the creek; that stretch and the bench on to the summit cut are graded measured. The route north of 2.25, on or beside the present highway, is still inferred: which side of the highway the rails ran is not settled.
