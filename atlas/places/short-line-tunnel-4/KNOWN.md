# Short Line Tunnel No. 4 (the Granite Tunnel): what is known and how well

Generated from the atlas records by `atlas/tools/summary.py`. Do not edit by hand; change the records and run it again.

The longest of the nine tunnels on the Colorado Springs and Cripple Creek District Railway, the Short Line, cut through granite without timbering: 521 ft. The line opened in April 1901 and was given up about 1920; its grade became a toll road and then Gold Camp Road. A curved timber trestle led to one portal.

## Still open

- The McClure photograph (MCC-83) shows the south portal: its camera solves there to 12 px, and only that portal has a hollow for the trestle. The north portal was not put through the solver, because the ground there has no hollow at all. The camera is good only to about 150 ft.
- The tunnel numbering: sources disagree on which tunnels survive and how they are numbered. No. 4 here rests on the length alone.
- Dates of the line (opened April 1901, abandoned about 1920 to 1922) are from secondary sources and need a primary one.
- The portals read off the lidar are about 440 ft apart, against 521 ft in the library caption and about 530 ft on the map trace; the approach cuts may hide the true portals.
- The trestle carried from the photograph to the ground comes out about 500 ft long and up to 70 ft high, but its far end misses the grade by about 45 ft, so its course is good only to about 100 ft. A second photograph, or the trestle footings on the ground, would fix it.
- The portal was taken as 16 ft wide and 19 ft from rail to crown to give the photograph its scale. That is an assumption, not a measurement; the bore still exists and could be measured.
