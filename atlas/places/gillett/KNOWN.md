# Gillett: what is known and how well

Generated from the atlas records by `atlas/tools/summary.py`. Do not edit by hand; change the records and run it again.

A Midland Terminal Railway town in Teller County, Colorado, platted in 1894 on the flats north-east of Cripple Creek. It peaked about 1896 to 1897, held the bullfight of August 1895 in a timber ring inside its racetrack, and faded after 1900. Nothing stands today but the ruin of the Catholic church.

- Street side and north end: **conjecture**. No photograph shows them.

**Bull ring**

- Size: **measured**. 150 ft arena in the Rocky Mountain News, 23 Aug 1895; photographs 8 to 9 times as wide as high.
- Position: **measured**. Second solve of the hill photograph X-8601; good to about 50 ft, lens angle unsettled.

**Catholic church (St. Joseph's)**

### What each rests on

- **Depot platform** (measured): Footprint and position from the November 1896 insurance sheet, 50 ft to the inch.
- **Freight house** (measured): Footprint and position from the November 1896 insurance sheet, 50 ft to the inch.
- **Racetrack (half-mile)** (measured): A continuous low bank on the 2024 lidar, also visible on aerial imagery: 350 m by 150 m. A line about 10 m inside it measures half a mile, matching the half-mile track named in the 1895 photographs.
- **Unidentified pad near the church** (measured): A levelled pad about 20 m across, 35 m west-north-west of the church remains, turned about 30 degrees to the street grid. Could be a later stock pond or building pad.
- **Lake below the Monte Carlo** (measured): The 1951 USGS Pikes Peak quadrangle draws a lake here, about 260 m by 75 m, in a side valley just east of town. The lidar shows its dam at the south end, and a redrawn 1898 plan puts 'Monte Carlo Lake' in the same place. Very likely the 12-acre lake J.H. Wolfe finished in January 1895, though no source says so outright. A dry meadow today. Outline scaled from the map, good to about 40 m.
- **Dam of the lake (breached)** (measured): A straight earth bank about 100 m long on the 2024 lidar, at the south end of the 1951 lake, with a deep gully cut through beside it and flood channels running out onto the flats. Whether this is the breach seen in the 1965 flood photographs is not settled: the City of Cripple Creek's reservoirs further up the valley also washed out that night.
- **Cripple Creek Reservoir No. 2 (main dam, washed out 1965)** (measured): State dam-safety GPS point, confirmed on the 1951 map. One of three City of Cripple Creek reservoirs on the West Fork of West Beaver Creek that washed out on 16 June 1965; rebuilt 1971.
- **Gillett Reservoir on Oil Creek (State's official name)** (measured): State dam ID 120240, built 1896, 25 ft high and 200 ft long, now breached. Probably the town waterworks reservoir. It drains away from Gillett Flats, so it did not cause the 1965 boulder field.
