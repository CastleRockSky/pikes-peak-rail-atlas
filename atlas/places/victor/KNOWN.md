# Victor: what is known and how well

Generated from the atlas records by `atlas/tools/summary.py`. Do not edit by hand; change the records and run it again.

Victor is the second town of the Cripple Creek district, on the south side of Battle Mountain. Three railways reached it. The Florence and Cripple Creek (3 ft gauge) lists it in its timetable of 2 June 1895, 5.7 miles before Cripple Creek. The Midland Terminal lists it in its timetable of 14 July 1895, 24 miles from Divide, and in every table read to March 1948; its brick depot at 4th Street and Granite Avenue is on the Sanborn sheets of 1900 and 1908, is photographed in the 1940s and was listed on the National Register in 1984. The Colorado Springs and Cripple Creek District (the Short Line) lists it in 1909, 44.5 miles from Colorado Springs, at the end of a branch from Cameron that Poor's Manual of 1902 gives as 5.13 miles. The Sanborn sheets of 1896, 1900 and 1908 show the Midland Terminal's main track passing the north-west corner of the town and its depot on a leg lettered 'Switchback', so that trains reversed to reach it (read from the lettering and the track plan); in December 1896 a freight car served as the depot, and the brick depot is on the sheet of August 1900. The Florence and Cripple Creek ran through the town south of Diamond Avenue to its own frame depot west of 3rd Street. Ten library photographs are held, of 1895 to about 1955.

## Still open

- Settled from the Sanborn sheets (24 Nov 2026): the brick depot's footprint and corner, and that it was built between December 1896 and August 1900, not in 1895. Whether it stands today has not been checked on the ground.
- Poley's 'Midland Terminal, Victor' (P-973, 1895-1899) shows a clapboarded frame station building. The Sanborn sheet of December 1896 shows NO Midland Terminal building in the town, only a freight car used as its depot at 5th and Granite, and the brick depot is there by August 1900. So P-973 is either a frame depot built and replaced between 1897 and 1900 (no sheet of those years was read), the Florence and Cripple Creek's depot (plain frame in 1896, iron clad by 1900) under a wrong title, or another place. Narrowed, not settled.
- The Pikes Peak postcard of about 1902 calls the brick depot 'Union Depot'. The Sanborn sheets do not show a shared depot: in 1900 the brick building is lettered 'M. T. Ry Depot' and in 1908 'Passenger Station' (the index: M. T. R. R.), and the Florence and Cripple Creek has its own depot in the same block, about 100 m south, in 1896, 1900 and 1908, on its own narrow-gauge track. The two railways were under common control from 1904 (Moody's, 1917), which might explain the name; that is a guess. Not settled.
- The Midland Terminal's switchback: where its leg left the main track is west of the sheets and not located. On the present ground the main track at the sheet's edge is 29 m above the leg's west end, so the meeting point was well to the west, perhaps the timetables' 'Switchback' 1.1 miles before Victor. The join drawn on the map there is conjecture.
- The last 0.6 mile of the Short Line's branch into Victor is not traced, and where the Florence and Cripple Creek and the Midland Terminal's 'main track' went east of Sanborn sheet 3 is not read.
- 'R. R. war in front of Strong mine' (1897): the library's explanation names a railway not yet built. What the event was is not established.
- Dates of the town itself (platting, incorporation, the fire of 1899) have not been researched: nothing about them is stated here.
- Photographs: found by 13 searches at Denver and 5 at Pikes Peak, first page of results each; not exhaustive. Two (X-546, X-547) were made after 1930; the owner decided on 24 November 2026 to show them. Not fetched: Otto Perry's 'MT depot' of 1935 (OP-12767), whose place is not established; a 1974 view of a station on Phantom Canyon Road that the library places at Alta Vista, 5 miles south (PPLD 002-3279).
