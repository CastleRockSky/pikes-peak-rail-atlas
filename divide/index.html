Divide

town, 1887 on

Divide is the town at the top of Ute Pass, where the Colorado Midland Railway crossed its summit on the way west from Colorado Springs and where the Midland Terminal Railway branched off south to the Cripple Creek district. Poor's Manual of 1887 says the Colorado Midland was finished 100 miles west from Colorado Springs by 1 July 1887, which takes in Divide, and Divide is a station in the Colorado Midland's public timetable of October 1888, 27.8 miles from Colorado Springs. The June 1895 timetable names it as the change for the Midland Terminal to Grassy and Cripple Creek, and the Midland Terminal's own tables count their miles from Divide (1895, 1909) and later from Colorado Springs, Divide being mile 26.9 (1920 to 1948, altitude printed 9,198 ft). Secondary sources say the station was first called Hayden's Divide, that the Midland Terminal was begun here in December 1893, that it took over the Colorado Midland's track east of Divide when that railway was given up in 1921 (a Gazette headline of 30 December 1921 reports the purchase), and that the last trains ran in 1949. The post office opened on 26 June 1889 (Wikipedia). The depot still stands south of US 24, about 0.4 mile east of the Highway 67 crossroads and next to the old junction; a 2025 newspaper article says it was built in 1886, burned a year later and was rebuilt in 1903, dates not yet checked against a primary record, and it is being restored. The 1894 and 1901 government maps draw the Colorado Midland climbing to the summit by a loop north of the wagon road, and the 1901 map draws a wye where the Midland Terminal leaves it. On the 1 m lidar the loop survives as a bench now used by Grandview Drive, the summit cut west of the crossroads is about 30 ft deep, and south of the junction the Midland Terminal's bank runs past the reservoir dam east of town and on along the creek. A straight cut beside the loop that the old maps do not show is unexplained. The junction and the wye are built over. The Denver Public Library holds a photograph of the earlier depot of about 1896 and Otto Perry's photographs of Midland Terminal trains near Divide in 1935 and 1949; a 1949 view by Ralph Hallock belongs to the Colorado Railroad Museum.

Still open

  • The depot's dates: the Gazette (2025) says built 1886, burned a year later, rebuilt 1903; 1904 is also given. 1886 is before the Colorado Midland is said to have reached Divide (1887). Needs a primary record (railway records, a newspaper of 1903 or 1904, the county's designation).
  • Whether the standing building is on the site of the depot in the c.1896 photograph (DPL X-7727), and where the 1887 depot stood: not established.
  • Where the junction switch and the wye's legs lay: the 1901 map draws a wye but at 1:125,000; the ground is built over. A faint line south-east of the junction is too steep on today's ground for track and is not taken as a leg.
  • Water tank, coal, section house, sidings or yard at Divide: no source seen mentions them. The 1901 map shows building symbols near the junction but not what they were.
  • The straight cut west of the crossroads, south of the loop: OpenStreetMap tags it Colorado Midland, but neither old map shows it and it climbs about 3.4 per cent. A line change after 1901, a siding or a road?
  • When the loop went out of use, and when the Colorado Midland west of Divide was taken up (1918 stop, 1921 abandonment: the secondary sources differ).
  • Mileage from Colorado Springs: 27.8 in the Colorado Midland's timetable of October 1888, 26.9 in the Midland Terminal's of 1920 to 1948, 27.4 on drgw.net. The difference may be a line change (the loop given up?) or a different measuring point; not resolved.
  • Altitude: the Midland Terminal timetables print 9,198 ft for Divide; the 1 m lidar ground is 9,112 ft at the depot and at most about 9,174 ft anywhere on the line inside the box (at the summit cut). Which point the 9,198 refers to is not known.
  • The depot is not in a timetable as a building; the timetables show only that Divide was a telegraph station from 1893 (a coupon station 1935 to 1940).
  • The 1894 and 1901 maps draw the loop about 800 ft east of where it lies on the ground; read only shapes from them here.
  • Photographs: the Denver Public Library and Pikes Peak Library District catalogues were searched by hand on 24 Nov 2026 with about eight keyword combinations (Divide with Midland, depot, station, train, Teller, Midland Terminal). DPL's search does not match phrases and 'Divide' is drowned by the Continental Divide (9,790 hits), so the search is not exhaustive. Where Perry's and Hallock's 'near Divide' views were taken has not been worked out. No image has been fetched.
  • Newspapers indexed by PPLD and not yet read: Gazette 30 Dec 1921 (Midland Terminal buys the track as far as Divide) and 23 May 1976 (the depot renovated).
  • Rights: eight of the catalogued photographs are of 1935 and 1949 (Otto Perry) and are marked as library copies that may be shown. That rests on the permission the owner reported, whose terms have not been seen; confirm it covers photographs still in copyright before any is fetched and shown.
